# Meter interpretation

The live detector keeps pulse tracking and meter interpretation separate. FFT, mel novelty, two tempo windows and the PLL still determine beats. A compact experimental consumer evaluates three/four-pulse cycles on the tracked grid. It is not a joint tempo/bar-pointer decoder or a neural downbeat model, and it does not identify notated denominators, arbitrary groupings or simultaneous meters.

Each matched onset retains raw novelty and normalized strength with its identity and sample position. Its accent is the maximum PCM power over the following FFT window. Three positive-novelty band sums are accumulated from the existing mel pass over the same window. A later unrelated emission hop cannot supply that onset's accent. Missing onsets contribute no fabricated feature vector. New meter fitting and band accumulation are disabled for manual/fixed counting; the legacy tracker still contributes to legacy confidence and lock behavior.

The consumer retains at most 32 beat observations. It transforms each available channel with `log1p(value / windowMean)` and standardizes channels with sufficient contrast. Three/four-position repeating means are compared against a common two-position null, so uniform accents or a two-pulse alias do not establish a bar. Both cycle models use the same observations and channel priors; an extra-mean penalty limits the advantage of the larger model. Channel evidence is marginalized so a noisy band cannot veto a consistent attack channel. The highest supported cycle and its phase anchor are evaluated separately: two equally plausible anchor positions leave phase unknown even when repetition supports a cycle.

This is an untrained periodicity/accent reference with explicit abstention. Its scores are heuristic, and its thresholds do not establish calibrated confidence or representative real-music accuracy. Synthetic correctness tests prove the tested associations, phase rules, transitions and event timing; they do not qualify the automatic method for a musical application. A release-quality automatic-method decision also requires independent annotated recordings and native runtime measurements. No model is selected merely because it ranks first among unsuccessful candidates.

Support lasts two tracked pulse periods plus one FFT finalization window after the latest supporting observation. This covers one missing pulse and its successor's complete attack without a tempo-independent timeout. Expiry removes model/phase availability, including in cached main-thread getters. It does not backdate a newly inferred downbeat event. Lookahead revisions can update after feature finalization without emitting another beat.

`MeterEstimate.selected` is null without a supported cycle. A selected model's `phasePulses` can independently be null. Alternatives describe competing cycle models rather than simultaneous meters. Reasons distinguish acquisition, insufficient evidence, low contrast, ambiguous cycle, ambiguous phase, availability and expiry. `meterKnown` requires a current selection and non-null phase; only then may downbeats and bar starts be emitted. Legacy display counters remain readable while meter is unknown.

Manual context is caller-owned musical/counting knowledge, copied at assignment. Phase refers to the first tracked beat of the new analysis epoch, not an automatically found acoustic bar. Configuration persists across reset/source changes; observations and anchors do not. Fixed four-pulse counting has origin `convention`; manual context has origin `manual`; audio-derived hypotheses have origin `estimated`. A score of 1 for a chosen rule describes that rule, not certainty about the music.

A forecast revision captures generation time, consumed-evidence time, grid period and provisional/locked status when generated. Later state delivery does not rewrite that metadata. The public immutable `schedule` view bounds the lifetime of all included interpretations; a snapshot containing downbeat flags cannot outlive meter support. A fresh beat-only view can remain available until pulse support expires. Regular state transport runs no faster than 20 Hz, rounded to whole analysis hops; beat, tempo and immediate reset notices remain separate.

Explicit EOF/pause notifications invalidate the epoch and stop audio consumption by the detector. Resume, reset or another source identity starts fresh. Notifications for seek, loop or starvation preserve an existing stop, as does manual reconfiguration. The tap does not infer these owner-level playback events from recorded silence.
